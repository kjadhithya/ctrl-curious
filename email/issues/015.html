>  10-minute brief
Designing for a browser that makes its own dark mode
Safari 27.3 beta, released on 3 November, can generate a dark version of any light website for people who use dark mode. The site is never told, so the dark page your users see may be one nobody on your team designed.
 Adaptive dark mode is a browser-made dark theme: Safari inverts a light page's luminosity while keeping its hues, then adjusts text towards a 4.5:1 contrast ratio.
What we know so far
1
WebKit's own explainer
Sites that declare light dark support are untouched, canvas is left alone, simple black and white bitmap logos may be inverted, and masks and blend modes can be switched off.
 
2
How people turn it on
Safari offers a Try prompt on bright sites when the system is in dark mode, and it can be enabled for all sites or just some; the release is expected in December.
 
3
Why you cannot measure it
Scripts that read colours get the authored values and canvas reads return the original, so analytics and brand checks only ever see the light design.
How to design for it
01If you already have a dark theme, declare it with color-scheme: light dark in the head or on :root, never later from JavaScript, so Safari leaves your design alone.
02If you want to stay light, plan for color-scheme: light only, but test the adapted version now, because beta 1 ignores the only keyword.
03Swap bitmap logos and icons for SVG and give content images transparent backgrounds; a baked-in white box stays bright on a darkened page.
04Find every effect built on mix-blend-mode or masks and make sure it still reads with the effect switched off.
05Check your brand colour on buttons and links in the adapted page: hue survives but lightness changes, and if it fails, ship your own dark palette with light-dark().
Why it matters to you
Plenty of marketing sites, docs and dashboards never got a dark theme because it was optional. Once this ships, Safari users in dark mode can see a version of your product you did not design and cannot track, so a deliberate choice now beats an accidental one later.
