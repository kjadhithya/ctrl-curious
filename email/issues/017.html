>  Today, in brief
YouTube's biggest navigation change in about a decade is reaching phones: the Subscriptions tab is gone from the bottom bar, now a swipe away inside Home, and early reaction is harsh, a reminder of what it costs to move a habit people rely on. Figma's third-quarter results put numbers on AI in design work: 65% of customers paying over $10K a year use the Figma agent weekly, and MCP use tripled in a quarter. Apple now lets apps show a retention message inside the App Store cancellation flow, which puts a new screen, and a new ethical line, in front of subscription designers. And two teams share what they learned building: Lovable on why it pulled autonomy back from its agent, Discord on the small craft rules behind its new video player.

01YouTube's app redesign drops the Subscriptions tab
9to5Google · The five-button bar gives way to one built around Shorts and creating, with Subscriptions moved to a swipe inside Home next to a custom feed; read it with the user reaction as a live case of platform goals overriding a well-worn habit.
02Figma's third quarter: two in three big customers use its agent every week
Figma, via StockTitan · Revenue rose 43% to $391.4M, over 80% of customers paying $10K or more used AI credits, MCP use tripled and writing back into files grew fourfold, and Dylan Field argues value is moving up the stack to design.
03Apple adds retention messages to the subscription cancellation flow
Apple Developer · Apps can now show text, images or a special offer when someone cancels, set who sees it and how often in App Store Connect, and pick a message per subscriber through a new API; worth deciding now where persuasion ends and obstruction starts.
04How we built Lovable, and why we keep rebuilding it
Lovable · Co-founder Anton Osika explains why fully autonomous agents wandered off, why they went back to a step-by-step flow people can review, and why short notes on recurring mistakes beat fine-tuning, lessons that apply to any AI feature you design.
05Modernizing the video playback experience at Discord
Discord · One player replaced four, with craft rules worth copying: no tap target under 24px from a small tile to fullscreen, tabular figures so the time doesn't jitter, its own clock for a smooth playhead, and tests written before the refactor.
>  Power-up
Impeccable gives your coding agent a design vocabulary and a check for AI-looking UI
Paul Bakaus's open-source skill, which grew out of Anthropic's frontend-design skill, adds 24 design commands to coding agents such as Claude Code, Cursor, Codex and Copilot. Version 4.8.0 arrived on 26 October, a week after 4.7.0 added two new typography checks, so it is a good moment to try it on a real screen.
Impeccable  Paul Bakaus · Free, Apache 2.0 licence
What it changes
●You steer with design words instead of long prompts: bolder, quieter, distill, typeset, layout, animate and more, plus polish, harden and adapt for the finishing pass.
●Critique gives a scored UX review and audit ranks accessibility, performance, theming and responsive issues from P0 to P3.
●Init writes a PRODUCT.md and document writes a DESIGN.md, so the agent keeps to your product and brand across sessions.
●A detector with 62 fixed rules, no AI or API key needed, flags tells such as Inter everywhere, purple-to-blue gradients, cards inside cards and grey text on colour.
Install it
Any supported agent, in your project's terminal
Run the installer; it finds the AI tools you use and asks whether to install for this project or globally. Run update later to get new versions.
npx impeccable install
Or, in Claude Code
Add the marketplace, then open /plugin and install Impeccable from there.
/plugin marketplace add pbakaus/impeccable
Then try asking
 Run /impeccable init once for the project, then ask: /impeccable critique the pricing cards.
 Our take  The commands are most useful once init has written PRODUCT.md, so spend five minutes on that first. Treat the detector's list as a review prompt rather than a verdict; some rules, like contrast, only run when it scans a live URL.
