Today, in brief
Figma now lets you rotate, tilt and add depth to layers natively (https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/44568893374999-Create-3D-effects-in-Figma-Design), keyframe it in Motion and copy the result as CSS in Dev Mode, so card flips and perspective effects no longer need a plugin or a hand-made spec. Smashing gives teams a way to argue about AI with evidence: a four-question audit (https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2026/11/should-you-add-ai-audit-product-teams/) that asks whether a feature saves more effort than it costs people to check it, and whether they can still spot its mistakes after a year. Two shipped redesigns are worth studying: Microsoft's Xbox PC app puts friends in the main navigation and merges search (https://mixed-news.com/en/xbox-pc-app-faster-preview-insiders-library/), and Apple TV treats sign language as a setting, like captions (https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/10/apple-tv-makes-sign-language-interpretation-a-built-in-accessibility-feature/), rather than a separate version of the film. For the weekend, Linear's Karri Saarinen makes the case for quality as strategy, and a new open-source skill called unslop (https://github.com/heydamir/unslop) teaches coding agents to fix AI-looking screens in a designer's order.
