Today, in brief
If you paste user comments straight into an AI design tool, NN/g's new study says you are leaving quality on the table: designs built from a researcher's synthesized findings met more criteria than ones built from raw feedback (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/raw-data-vs-synthesized-data/), and only the synthesized ones remembered loyalty miles. Jakob Nielsen rounds up research showing developers wave through agent permission prompts (https://www.uxtigers.com/post/ux-roundup-20261120) and proposes which actions should be silent, asked or blocked. On the interface side, Google has swapped its homepage's search button for Gemini shortcuts and a chat-history sidebar (https://www.engadget.com/2322647/google-latest-home-page-redesign-makes-gemini-even-more-prominent/), a sign that even the simplest query box is turning into a resumable session. And this week's skill comes from UX Collective: a way to sort dashboards so the fires come first (https://uxdesign.cc/send-the-firefighters-to-the-fires-6cd7b1893ae7).
