Today, in brief
Design work no longer has to stop at handoff: Google Labs' Stitch Loop watches a shipped product and opens pull requests (https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-labs/stitch-loop/) to fix accessibility, speed and security, with nothing merged until a person reviews it. Two UX Collective essays tell you how to stay in charge of that kind of output: Patrick Neeman makes the case that the prototype should come before the requirements doc (https://uxdesign.cc/prototypes-have-always-been-the-most-effective-specification-10162107b2ee), and Tania Ostanina lays out guardrails for cleaning up AI-made screens (https://uxdesign.cc/the-attack-of-the-giant-ai-design-slop-monster-a-survivors-guide-55f5918f78f4) from PMs and developers. On the shipped side, Claude's Android app now follows Material 3 Expressive (https://9to5google.com/2026/11/23/claude-android-material-3-update/) while keeping its own colours, and Rockstar explains how the GTA 6 HUD kept its heritage and gained accessibility settings (https://www.creativebloq.com/news/how-gta-6s-interface-kept-the-classic-pricedown-font-but-still-managed-to-feel-modern). If your files still mix old and new auto layout, Figma's switch-back option ends in January 2027 (https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/42031586813719), so today's tool to master is upgrading them safely.
