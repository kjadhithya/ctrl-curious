Today, in brief
Canva made its MCP server generally available yesterday and merged overlapping tools so even smaller AI models pick the right one (https://cxotoday.com/media-coverage/building-the-design-layer-of-the-internet-opening-canva-to-every-agent-and-developer/), while Figma's agent is now on every paid plan in FigJam and Slides (https://www.figma.com/blog/introducing-figma-agent-in-figjam-and-slides/), turning boards into specs and specs into on-brand decks; today's brief looks at what designing for an agent as a user actually involves. Going the other way, artist Stuart Semple launched NIVA, a free browser studio with no AI and no templates (https://www.techradar.com/pro/its-not-template-based-like-canva-it-has-no-ai-to-do-it-for-you-a-free-browser-studio-launches-without-subscriptions-or-automated-shortcuts). Tushar Deshmukh argues that one-box AI interfaces hide how much literacy they demand (https://uxmag.com/articles/the-new-illiteracy), with Indian data to back it, and Luke Wroblewski makes the case for throwaway design playgrounds built by coding agents (https://www.lukew.com/ff/2172/design-by-playground) instead of static mockups. And Cal.com's interface cut of its typeface, Cal Sans Text UI, was merged into the Google Fonts library (https://github.com/google/fonts/pull/11432) yesterday.
