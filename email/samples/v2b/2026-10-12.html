>  Skill of the week
Measure what users feel without NPS
A single score tells you little about what to fix. This method from boldstart, built at Snyk, scores a few specific statements instead and shows you where the gap is.
01Write up to six statements about the product, such as "It is intuitive and easy to use", and never change the wording between rounds.
02Ask people to rate each from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5, with an optional free-text box.
03Average each statement and plot them as spokes on a radar chart so weak areas stand out.
04Pick the lowest one and set a target for next quarter, for example from 1.75 to 3.5.
05Split results by plan, team size and new versus long-time users, and repeat the survey each quarter.
 Our take  Most useful for B2B and tools, where one recommendation score hides very different experiences.
 
>  Prompt of the week
Turn interview notes into an empathy map
NN/g has refreshed its guide to empathy mapping. Paste your interview notes or transcripts into an assistant and try this to get a first draft in minutes:
Prompt  ·  Any AI assistant
> Here are notes from [number] user interviews about [topic]. Build an empathy map for [user type] with four parts: Says, Thinks, Does, Feels. Only use what is in the notes. After each point, add the participant number and the line it comes from. Then list the three biggest gaps between what people say and what they do. Mark anything that came up only once.
Check every point against the notes before you share it, and build the final map with your team.
Use it for
—  A first draft from a pile of notes
—  Spotting say-do gaps quickly
 
Not for
—  Replacing synthesis with your team
—  Filling gaps with guesses
