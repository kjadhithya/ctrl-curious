A design review agenda that ends in decisions
Parvaneh Toghiani, a principal designer at Uber, uses a simple structure to keep reviews from circling. Copy it into your next review deck or doc:
review-agenda.md
Review type: alignment / options / sign-off Pre-read: sent 24 hours before 1. Goals and non-goals (2 min) 2. Decisions already made (1 min) 3. Decision criteria, agreed before any design is shown 4. Options: about three, from practical to ambitious, with our recommendation in the middle 5. Discussion: note feedback live, park side topics Recap: each goal as a question, marked decided or open, with owner and next step
Present for about half the meeting and leave the rest for discussion. Never show more than five options.

>  10-minute brief
Delegation UI, explained
Three launches this week share one idea: the product does the work, and the interface is where you hand it over and check it.
 The controls that let people hand a job to software, follow its progress and confirm or correct the result, instead of doing each step themselves.
Where it showed up this week
1
Hand it over
Gemini's Chat or Task switch makes delegating a visible choice, and tasks keep running when you leave.
 
2
Confirm or correct
Fitbit asks "Tennis detected" with Confirm and Something else, so fixing a wrong guess takes one tap.
 
3
Steer as you go
Ask YouTube keeps a follow-up box under the results so people refine instead of starting over.
Why it matters to you
More of your screens will show work the product did on its own. Designing the handover, the progress and the undo is now part of the job.
